Seven Cures for a Lean Purse

First Cure: Start thy purse to fattening

Arkad’s first cure speaks about saving. For every ten coins that go into your purse, spend only nine. He explains it using an egg merchant who adds ten eggs to a basket every morning and then removes nine in the evening, so the basket slowly overflows. What stuck with me is Arkad's confession that even after doing this, he got along just as well as before. This suggests that we spend more on things that we will never miss. It also shows that saving doesn't start when you think you have enough; saving comes first and your lifestyle should fit around it.

APPLY TO MY LIFE:

Whenever I receive money, I will save 10% before I spend any of it. Even a small amount helps me to build the habit, and I can increase the percentage as my income grows, reducing the amount of money that I waste.

Second Cure: Control thy expenditures

The second cure emphasizes the need to distinguish between necessities and desires. Arkad explains that expenses tend to increase as income increases unless a person deliberately controls their spending. When a student objects that a budget could enslave him, Arkad answers that a budget is a light in the dark cave. It exposes where the money leaks out so that it can be spent on purposes we actually need.

APPLY TO MY LIFE:

I have learned that earning more money doesn't automatically lead to financial success. Without proper money management, I could spend everything that I earn and still experience financial difficulties. I plan to implement this lesson by tracking my expenses and eliminating financial leaks, prioritizing necessities such as food and transportation while limiting unnecessary spending such as entertainment. Before I buy something, I will first decide if it is essential or just another poor spending decision. This will help me to avoid wasteful spending and become more disciplined with money.

Third Cure: Make thy gold multiply

Saved gold that sits idle only satisfies a miser. Arkad says that the true measure of wealth is the income you build that flows whether you are working or traveling. He showed this with a story about a farmer who left ten pieces of silver with a moneylender for his newborn son, asking him to reinvest the interest as it accumulated. Through this compounding process, the initial ten pieces of silver grew to more than one hundred and sixty by the time the son turned fifty.

APPLY TO MY LIFE:

Building on this idea, Cure Three taught me that money should be used as a tool that creates future opportunities rather than sitting idle. Once my savings reach a solid baseline, I plan to invest a portion into a growth option, such as shares, and before I commit to any financial product or seek professional advice, I will make sure that I understand how interest, inflation, and risk affect my money.

Fourth Cure: Guard thy treasures from loss

In Cure Four, Arkad tells a story about his own mistake, where he gave a year's savings to a brickmaker who promised to buy jewels in Tyre but came home with worthless glass. This shows the importance of protecting your money from poor investments, dishonest individuals, and decisions made without sufficient knowledge.

APPLY TO MY LIFE:

I have learned that the desire to become rich quickly can lead to serious financial mistakes. I should not invest just because someone promises unusually high returns or because an opportunity looks popular. Instead, I will research all the risks involved and consult professionals.

Fifth Cure: Make of thy dwelling a profitable investment

In the fifth cure, Arkad urges people to own a home instead of paying rent to a landlord forever. He notes that lenders will willingly provide a mortgage once the buyer provides a down payment for the property. Ownership brings stability, pride, and a valuable asset, allowing money that would have been spent on rent to build equity instead.

APPLY TO MY LIFE:

Building on the previous lessons, one of my long-term goals is to become a homeowner. To work toward this, I will save consistently, manage my debt responsibly, and educate myself on property costs, mortgages, and maintenance. I believe that by implementing the principles from Cures One through Four, achieving homeownership becomes a realistic goal.

Sixth Cure: Insure a future income

In Cure Six, Arkad reminds the class that every life moves from youth to old age, and a wise person prepares for the years when their earning power fades and for the family that might be left behind. His example is the sandal-maker Ansan, who deposited just two pieces of silver every week and ended up with a comfortable sum for his retirement.

APPLY TO MY LIFE:

This cure teaches me that financial planning should not focus only on the present, as I need to consider my future responsibilities and prepare for unexpected circumstances. I will apply these principles by building an emergency fund, securing proper insurance coverage, and contributing toward a retirement plan. Planning early reduces the risk of becoming a financial burden on my family and ensures my financial independence as I grow older.

Seventh Cure: Increase thy ability to earn

The final cure focuses on self-improvement rather than just managing money. Arkad explains that achievement requires a clear and specific desire. For example, simply wishing to be rich yields no result, whereas setting a concrete goal to earn five pieces of gold creates a target that can be actively pursued and achieved. He illustrates this by contrasting a young man who constantly begged his master for a raise with his own experience as a scribe, where sharpening his skills caused his pay to increase naturally without begging. Arkad also outlines the principles of a respectable life, which include paying debts promptly, caring for family, making a will, and helping those in need.

APPLY TO MY LIFE:

This cure is the most relevant to my current stage in life. As a Cybersecurity student, I will treat my skills as my primary investment by taking on new tasks to build a portfolio, earning industry-level certifications, and securing internships. I will also focus on improving soft skills, such as communication, problem-solving, and time management, which are essential for professional success. I believe that by continuously investing in myself, I can enhance my career prospects and better position myself to achieve my financial goals.

Five Laws of Gold

The First Law of Gold

The first law states that gold comes gladly and increases in quantity for anyone who consistently puts aside at least one-tenth of their earnings to build an estate for themselves and their family.

APPLY TO MY LIFE:

Similar to Cure One, I have learned that saving must become a habitual practice rather than something I only do when I have extra money. I will apply this law by saving at least 10% of my income whenever possible and gradually increasing that percentage as my financial situation improves.

The Second Law of Gold

Gold is a willing worker that multiplies “even as the flocks of the field” when its owner finds profitable employment for it. The second law explains that money grows when it is put to productive use through wise investment instead of allowing savings to remain unproductive.

APPLY TO MY LIFE:

This law has helped to me understand the difference between simply having money and using it effectively. Going forward, I will view every dollar as a resource to generate more wealth, because idle money earns nothing and slowly loses its value over time. I will educate myself on financial investment opportunities and consider which ones are worth investing in.

The Third Law of Gold

The third law teaches that gold remains with the cautious owner who seeks advice from people who understand how to manage and invest money. Financial decisions should be guided by knowledge and experience instead of hopes and guesses.

APPLY TO MY LIFE:

I have learned that I do not need to know everything about money, but I must be willing to learn from trustworthy and knowledgeable people. Before I make any financial decisions, I will ask qualified professionals for relevant information. I will also research information from reliable sources and ask questions whenever I do not understand something. This will reduce the chance of me making poor decisions that could ruin my financial future.

The Fourth Law of Gold

The fourth law warns that gold can slip from people who invest in businesses or purposes they don't understand, or when they ignore the advice of wise people.

APPLY TO MY LIFE:

This law reminds me not to follow others blindly when making financial decisions. For example, if someone encourages me to invest in a business simply because it appears profitable, I should first understand how the business operates, how it earns money, and what risks are involved. I will avoid committing my savings to something I cannot explain or properly evaluate.

The Fifth Law of Gold

The fifth law warns that gold flees from the person who forces it to earn impossible returns, follows the advice of tricksters and schemers, or trusts only their own inexperience or unrealistic expectations.

APPLY TO MY LIFE:

I have learned that the desire for quick success can make people vulnerable to scams or financial exploitation. To prevent this, I will be careful when someone tries to pressure me into investing, guarantees me huge profits, or offers me returns that are too good to be true. Before making an investment, I will verify with multiple sources whether the offer is real or a scam.

My Biggest Takeaway

The main lesson I learned from the book is that consistent saving and disciplined budgeting must work together. Financial success depends far less on total income than on how much money is retained and put to work. When earnings increase, living expenses should not automatically rise alongside them. Instead, the focus must remain on eliminating wasteful spending and securing long-term stability. Saving alone cannot build true wealth, which makes investing in growth opportunities necessary. Furthermore, because all investments carry risk, the book emphasizes that conducting thorough research and seeking professional guidance are essential steps before making any major financial decision.
